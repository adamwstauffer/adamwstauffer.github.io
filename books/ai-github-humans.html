AI + GitHub + Humans = Future of Work: A Rethinking of Higher Education Curriculum

Courses for a world where AI drafts the first pass: students frame, specify, build with AI, audit and decide, in the open on GitHub. It starts from the doer–reviewer dilemma.

Adam W. Stauffer · Cases in progress

The idea

The career ladder used to run from college to an entry-level job, to on-the-job training, to manager. What a person contributed climbed with it: from knowledge, to doing, to specific expertise, to reviewing other people's work. The years of doing were where reviewers were made.

AI now does much of that doing, and the entry-level rung is the one it removes. So how does anyone become a reviewer when a machine does the doing? This book's answer: bring the doer stage into the classroom. Students do the work with AI, keep a record of it, then review it — specify, build, audit, defend — on every assignment, so the apprenticeship the job market no longer offers at entry level happens before graduation, with the evidence committed. Some of the doing has to be done without AI, too. Otherwise the student never becomes a reviewer either.

It starts from the doer–reviewer dilemma.

Who it is for

Students, professors and administrators. It is not a manual. It is one possible path to using AI well in business and in the classroom — the route I took, with the forks marked where someone else might turn.

The path

Draft. Stages and titles will change.

  3. 3Ready-made skills and agentsRun someone else's tooling before you write your own.
  4. 4GitHubPut the work where it has a history.
  5. 5Your own skills and agents, in GitHubTurn the task you repeat into a skill, a command, then an agent, and version it with the work.
  6. 6GitHub + a databaseAdd a memory layer beside the record.

Questions colleagues actually ask, such as "What if the AI is wrong?" and "Is this cheating?", appear as sidebars at the step where the answer becomes concrete. So do short cases from marketing, finance and economics.

The cases behind the book are running in my courses now. The book follows from what they show.
