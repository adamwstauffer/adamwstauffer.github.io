The 2026 Strait of Hormuz Closure: An Extended Case in Microeconomics

Supply and demand, costs, market structure and externalities, taught through one live shock to the world's oil supply.

Adam W. Stauffer · Lecture decks built; book in progress

The idea

Every tool in an introductory microeconomics course is needed to understand one event, the 2026 closure of the Strait of Hormuz. Each chapter earns its place by explaining a part of that event the previous chapter could not.

Microeconomics is usually taught one model at a time, each with its own toy example, and students rarely see the models work together. Real shocks don't arrive one model at a time. The closure moved prices, costs, market power, externalities and trade in the same weeks. This book starts with the shock, builds the machinery, then uses it on power, market failure and the border, and each chapter comes back to the strait with a sharper tool.

How each chapter works

  • Reading. The news that opens the chapter.
  • Puzzle and predict. A question you commit to before the tool is taught.
  • Reason. The chapter's microeconomics.
  • Worksheet. The arithmetic, done by hand.
  • Reveal. What actually happened in the strait, and how well the prediction did.

The case is still moving, so every reveal is dated: what was known, as of when.
