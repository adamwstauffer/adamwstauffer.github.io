The 2008 Global Financial Crisis: An Extended Case in Macroeconomics

Told through the crisis chronology and from the ETF desk at Lehman Brothers and then Barclays Capital, where I traded through it.

Adam W. Stauffer · Lecture decks built; book in progress

The idea

Every tool in an introductory macroeconomics course — inflation, unemployment, GDP, valuation, money, policy, the exchange rate — was stress-tested in public during the Global Financial Crisis. This book reads each one as a trading desk lived it in 2007–2009: one date at a time, knowing only what had printed that morning.

Textbooks show the revised data and hide the decision. A desk never sees the economy whole or on time. Data arrive as first prints, get revised later, and decisions get made on the version that turns out to be wrong. Each chapter here opens on a dated morning, teaches the chapter's macroeconomics straight, and then shows what the revisions and the following months revealed.

How each chapter works

  • The date and the desk. The morning, the event that defined it, and what the desk was doing.
  • The snapshot. Nine numbers as they printed that day: payrolls, unemployment, jobless claims, GDP, house prices, the fed funds rate, the 10-year yield, the yield curve and the S&P 500.
  • The markets. Stocks, short and long rates, and oil over the trailing year.
  • The tool. The chapter's macroeconomics.
  • What happened next. First print against the revised number, and the road to the next chapter's date.
